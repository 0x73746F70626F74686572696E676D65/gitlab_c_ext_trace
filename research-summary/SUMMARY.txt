This is research on saved results only. No GitLab application, endpoint,
extension, analysis generator, or vulnerability test was run. The exports copy
existing entrypoint fields and group existing records; they do not establish new
authentication conclusions.

WHAT THE PRINCIPAL SAVED INVENTORY SAYS

The most comprehensive saved authentication-specific inventory is
unauthenticated_entrypoints/http_unauthenticated.json. It contains 2,420 HTTP
registration records marked anonymous_access=true:

  688 have the saved classification unauthenticated.
  1,732 have the saved classification conditional_anonymous.
  1,076 are CE records and 1,344 are EE records.
  They contain 1,196 distinct literal path-template strings and 1,295 distinct
  method/path pairs when editions, handlers, and duplicate registrations are
  collapsed. This is a count of the saved declarations, not deployed URLs.
  There are 245 handler families.

By registration type:
  1,695 Rails action records
    621 Grape/API endpoint records
     96 other HTTP registrations, including redirects and inline responses
      4 dedicated health-listener records
      2 health middleware records
      2 WebSocket transport registrations

CE/EE repetitions and organization-prefixed aliases account for many repeated
records. /o/:organization_path/... is a separately recorded alias. Main-listener
paths also need the installation's configured relative_url_root. ANY and ALL
are saved wildcard declarations; UNKNOWN is an unresolved method declaration.
No implicit HEAD/OPTIONS expansion was added during this research.

The full authentication partitions additionally contain 12 anonymous-labeled
error-only HTTP records that the saved public/application lists intentionally
exclude. They are also preserved here, in error-only-http-records.csv: ANY
/api/v3/*path and /api/v4/*path, GET /-/jira_connect, POST /jwt/auth, and the
organization aliases for the latter two, across CE/EE. They document handlers
that return errors, not admission to a useful application operation. Thus 2,432
HTTP records have anonymous auth labels when these 12 error handlers are included.

The saved inventory explicitly says complete=false and runtime_verified=false.
Its labels describe source-supported, bounded review. They do not prove that
every listed operation is admitted from an arbitrary anonymous request. An
ordinary helper/service, resource policy, credential check, deployment setting,
middleware, feature flag, or route precedence can affect admission. Records such
as profile operations, runner/internal APIs, uploads, token exchange, and
admin-named fields must therefore be read as saved classifications, not as
newly confirmed unrestricted access. This qualification comes from the saved
inventory's own README, metadata, and coverage data.

DOCUMENTED HTTP FAMILIES

The complete records, methods, handlers, source locations, saved conditions,
and evidence IDs are in http-records.csv. Every one of the 2,420 records is
retained. http-method-paths.csv gives all 1,295 distinct method/path pairs.
http-handler-families.csv lists every handler and its recorded actions.

1. Sign-in, registration, recovery, confirmation and identity-verification
   entrypoints. The saved list includes GET /users/sign_in, GET /users/sign_up,
   password new/edit/update routes, confirmation and unlock routes, passkey
   sign-in initiation, email-OTP fallback and verification-related actions.
   Authentication-form access and successful credential submission are separate
   records; this summary does not promote every submission to anonymous success.
   Devise-generated routes and organization aliases are retained.

2. OAuth/OIDC and discovery. Recorded families include dynamic registration
   POST /oauth/register(.:format), OAuth token/introspection/revocation handlers,
   token-info and Geo-auth handlers, JWKS, /.well-known/openid-configuration,
   /.well-known/oauth-authorization-server, /.well-known/webfinger,
   /.well-known/oauth-protected-resource, and their recorded MCP/Orbit variants.
   Related metadata includes /.well-known/security.txt,
   /.well-known/terraform.json and /.well-known/change-password.
   Form/session independence does not remove any separately required token,
   client credential, certificate, or callback verification.

3. Public project browsing and collaboration. Conditional records cover project
   overview/activity, repository tree/blob/raw/blame/find-file views, commits,
   branches, tags, comparisons and diffs, issues and notes/discussions, merge
   requests and their related views, labels, milestones, snippets, wikis,
   releases, badges, forks/starrers, selected project membership/autocomplete,
   analytics and environment/deployment views. Public resource visibility and
   the recorded read abilities/settings are conditions. Representative templates
   include /*namespace_id/:id, /*namespace_id/:project_id/-/branches,
   /*namespace_id/:project_id/-/blob/*id and their aliases.

4. CI/build information. The list includes selected pipeline/job/build, test,
   artifact, schedule and metrics handlers, plus Grape pipeline/runner/trigger
   registrations. These retain their saved labels, which do not establish that
   job tokens, runner credentials or private-project authorization are absent.
   GET /api/v4/projects/:id/ci/lint is recorded as conditional public-resource
   access. The older POST CI-lint input-flow recipe is a different record and
   is not automatically an anonymous operation.

5. REST public-resource families. The Grape inventory includes selected projects,
   groups, users, avatars, topics, templates, repositories, branches/tags,
   commits/files, releases/release links, issues/merge requests, snippets,
   events, work-item and analytics registrations. Conditions and exact verbs
   vary by record. The exported handler table also retains less intuitive
   source-labeled families such as internal, integration, SCIM, compliance,
   security and remote-development registrations without certifying access.

6. Package and registry reads. Saved records cover Cargo, Composer, Conan v1/v2,
   Debian project/group packages and distributions, Generic packages, Go proxy,
   Helm, Maven, npm project/group/instance endpoints and dist-tags, NuGet
   project/group endpoints, PyPI, RPM, RubyGems and Terraform modules.
   Container repository/tag views, dependency proxies and virtual-registry
   handlers also appear. Anonymous package reads depend on public visibility,
   package features and the recorded package-read policy. Upload/write and
   token-bearing operations are not inferred public from a family name.

7. Git HTTP, LFS and downloads. Repository discovery/transport declarations,
   LFS API/storage/locks, project/snippet artifacts and upload-serving handlers
   appear with recorded methods and constraints. Upload paths containing secret
   placeholders are preserved as templates; they do not establish permission
   for an arbitrary file or imply that no secret is required.

8. ActivityPub. The saved list includes project release index/outbox and POST
   /*namespace_id/:project_id/-/releases/inbox. The older independently reviewed
   inbox record specifically requires enabled ActivityPub/project flags, a
   public nondeleting project, read_project permission, normal routing/content
   negotiation and admitted callbacks/middleware. The recorded login-filter
   skip is not a universal access statement.

9. Information pages, exploration and small public services. Saved families
   include help/documentation/instance configuration, project-topic and group
   exploration, the CI catalog and snippets, sitemap, countries/states,
   WhatsNew, ACME challenge, Cloud Connector keys, redirects, unsubscribe and
   invitation handlers, sandbox renderers, diagram proxies, subscription/trial
   pages and selected Jira Connect descriptors/keys/callback routes.
   Some are informational pages or redirects; a saved classification does not
   establish admission to every related callback or integration operation.

10. Event ingestion and request infrastructure. POST /-/collect_events is
    recorded, alongside other explicit HTTP redirects/inline responses and
    router/cache registrations. Saved compression/body-validation records
    describe middleware phases and do not each supply a new external route.

11. Health and monitoring. GET /-/liveness and /-/readiness are recorded on the
    main listener; /liveness and /readiness also have separate dedicated-listener
    registrations. /-/health has a recorded client-IP allowlist condition.
    /-/metrics and /-/metrics/system appear with the source inventory's labels.
    Enabled listeners, network access and monitoring settings remain material.

12. GraphQL and WebSocket transport. GET/POST /api/graphql is the HTTP transport.
    The explorer/GLQL-related registrations are separately recorded. GET
    /-/cable is a recorded WebSocket handshake, not proof that an anonymous
    connection can use a channel or subscribe to a GraphQL subscription.

GRAPHQL COUNTS ARE SEPARATE FROM HTTP URL COUNTS

graphql_unauthenticated.json contains 3,006 source-labeled anonymous GraphQL
records: 170 root query records and 2,836 nested field records. Of these, 614
are labeled unauthenticated and 2,392 conditional_anonymous. All share the
GraphQL HTTP transport; they are not 3,006 additional URLs. CE contributes
1,190 and EE 1,816 records. No mutation or subscription is included in this
saved public GraphQL partition.

graphql-records.csv retains every field/query, schema witness, resolver, edition,
condition and source reference. Witness paths are saved shortest source paths,
not proof that a runtime object or policy permits traversing every field.
currentUser's two edition records explicitly have nullable_identity responses;
they do not establish disclosure of a logged-in identity to an anonymous user.
Framework-generated connection, edge, interface and introspection members are
not fully expanded in the saved inventory.

OLDER SAVED CLAIMS, CORRECTIONS AND REPEATED REFERENCES

The earlier frontend_gem_api_map inventory has 331 controller candidates labeled
unauthenticated. This is not a second list of 331 verified anonymous routes.
Every such candidate is retained in legacy-anonymous-candidates.csv, with its
original routes/source location and any existing targeted-review result.
Handler/action comparison to the later inventory is only a lookup of existing
labels; no route equivalence or new authentication result is inferred.

The saved targeted review of 191 original candidates reports:
  142 registered actions;
   42 public methods with unresolved HTTP registration;
    7 callback blocks previously miscounted as actions;
   27 retained unauthenticated classifications and 164 unknown classifications.

The 27 include two ActivityPub base helpers without reconstructed HTTP actions.
Examples of recorded public actions in that review include ActivityPub release
actions, OAuth dynamic/protected-resource metadata, Jira Connect descriptors,
sandbox renderers, unsubscribe/redirect handlers, Terraform discovery,
security.txt, WhatsNew, CustomersDot proxy, Kerberos negotiation, Telesign
callback and sitemap. Their conditions remain attached to the original records.

The legacy baseline also calls some actions public that the newer saved
inventory marks unknown or authenticated. For example, RootController#index is
an older unauthenticated candidate but the newer '/' registration is unknown;
Groups::WorkItemsController#show and Projects::Aws::ConfigurationController#index
have newer authenticated labels. This is why an indiscriminate union would
misrepresent the saved evidence. All older claims remain in the legacy table.

The newer receiver/argument companions contain 122 rows selected by existing
anonymous labels, referring to 82 existing entrypoint IDs and 44 path strings.
All 82 IDs resolve to the principal inventory; the labels agree. These repeated
downstream references add no entrypoints. downstream-anonymous-references.csv
retains the IDs and source-table record numbers without interpreting downstream
operations. The recorded downstream source revision differs from the principal
inventory revision; that discrepancy is retained in the original metadata.

The depth-limited search also retains 5,438 anonymous-labeled root records:
3,704 were walked and 1,734 have unresolved roots. Every root ID resolves to
the principal inventory and the saved auth labels agree. This is exactly the
5,426 public/application records plus 12 error-only records, not another set
of routes. Its 346,088 anonymous-labeled method-name-match rows do not create
new authentication conclusions. Larger match/decision/unresolved-stop tables
were included in the full saved-file search, including compressed tables.

OLDER REQUEST-FLOW ARCHIVES AND AUTHENTICATION LIMITS

The compact CURRENT_INPUT_FLOWS bundle has 29 saved reference records: 26 main,
two partial and one auxiliary. They are conditional request-processing recipes,
not an anonymous-endpoint census. Their selected source families are OAuth
dynamic registration, event forwarding, ActivityPub inbox, branches, GraphQL
package cursor, passkey registration/password/JSON phases, compressed JSON
middleware, Slack options/interactions, CI lint, MLflow artifact paths and
semantic-search settings. Multiple records repeat the same external route.

The saved notes specifically keep Slack request signatures, internal shared
secrets, admin settings permissions, passkey authenticated-user requirements,
MLflow project/auth permissions and Conan package/schema permissions as gates.
Those recipes are not counted here as universally anonymous endpoints. The
passkey Rails parsing notes describe processing before local controller auth
under prior middleware admission, and expressly avoid a general anonymous-
access claim. No memory-operation paths were reanalyzed for this report.

The broad frozen/iteration graph counts, native registration counts and lexical
method matches are not additional anonymous entrypoints. Their READMEs explicitly
distinguish candidates, contradicted paths, partials, conditional exclusions and
input-flow recipes. The newest full-graph checkpoint is unfinished and produced
no final joined reachability inventory. Its serialized parser state and
interrupted native streams were included in the saved-material search; no state
was executed or used to derive new authentication findings.

Older registration tables also record global middleware such as
Gitlab::Middleware::UnauthenticatedSessionExpiry. A middleware call registration
has no separately established HTTP URL and does not add a public route.

WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED IN THE SAVED DATA

The main inventory has 19,671 unknown records, including 4,024 HTTP records;
unknown is not a private or public classification. Coverage retains 554 routing-
snapshot templates with unresolved methods/handlers/authentication, 103 Workhorse
dispatch registrations with delegated/unresolved authentication, 4,057 older
baseline records without reconstructed registrations, and explicit parse,
dynamic-dispatch, route, engine and policy gaps. The 5,058-template saved EE
routing snapshot is fully accounted for, but only 4,504 templates are matched
to source registrations. These counts describe the saved inventory's bounds.

All saved public claims are represented in the principal/legacy/reference
tables. The evidence does not support an exhaustive runtime-verified list of
everything any anonymous visitor can successfully use on a deployed GitLab.
Research coverage and any unreadable stream boundaries are recorded separately
in RESEARCH_COVERAGE.txt and research-coverage.json.
